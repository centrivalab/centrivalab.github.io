Batch Planner

A batch process — concentration, diafiltration, solvent exchange, or a sequence of them — sweeps the product concentration from start to finish. The six tubes of one CentrivaLab run are six instants of that batch: each reproduces the flux and the membrane-surface concentrations the module sees at that point. Describe the batch and the module, get the six feeds to prepare and the speed to run; enter the results and the batch is re-integrated with the membrane's measured response — time, yield, diafiltration optimum.

Model & validity

Batch. Well-mixed tank recirculated through the module; the tank state is integrated in permeate-volume steps (1/400 of the batch volume). Concentration removes permeate; diafiltration adds fresh solvent at the permeate rate (constant volume). Product loss is the integral of the permeate concentration.

Module. 1-D plug flow along the feed channel as in the Test Planner: Schock & Miquel (1987) mass transfer per species, film model, van't Hoff osmotic pressure of both species (× the osmotic factor), channel friction. Feed viscosity rises linearly with product concentration by the entered coefficient; diffusivities follow Stokes–Einstein at that viscosity.

Second species. An impurity is a second solute with its own rejection and mass transfer. In a solvent exchange the old solvent permeates freely (its fraction decays as e−N), and the mixture density is linear, ln μ linear, in the volume fraction — a first approximation; the tubes measure what the mixture actually does.

Cartridge. Buoyancy-driven polarisation model of Geraldes et al. (2026); the product drives the buoyancy, the impurity is treated as a dilute tracer with its own diffusivity. Each tube's feed is solved so that the run-mean wall concentrations of product (and impurity) equal the module's at that batch state; one speed is shared, set from the mean ΔP and mean feed density, and the ΔP of each tube at its own density is reported.

Diafiltration optimum. The diafiltration time for N diavolumes is N·mP/(A·J(c)·c): it is shortest where J·c is largest (Ng, Lundblad & Mitra, Sep. Sci. Technol. 11 (1976) 499; the "DF optimisation parameter C·Jf" of the Millipore TFF brief). Under gel polarisation this is clim/e; here J(c) comes from the module model and, after the run, from the tubes. In a solvent exchange the curve is measured in both solvents. The analytical checks shown use constant observed rejection: product loss = 1 − e(R−1)(ln VCF + N), contaminant remaining = e(R−1)N.

Validity. Van't Hoff underestimates the osmotic pressure of concentrated solutions: use the osmotic factor from a measured value. The O-CMF correlation was validated with aqueous salts; in organic solvents it is an extrapolation — the two-concentration signature test in the Test Planner guide checks it. Solvents denser than the product (β ≤ 0) are outside the model.

Experimental guide — batch runs

How to run six tubes as six instants of a batch. Preparation times and speeds depend on your membrane and solvent — they are not calculated here.

Six feeds from one stock

  • Prepare a product stock at or above the highest cartridge feed concentration in the run sheet and dilute gravimetrically to each tube's concentration; with an impurity, add it from a separate stock to each tube's value.
  • Measure the actual concentration of each feed — it is the C0 used when reading the results.
  • In a solvent exchange, feeds in the initial and final solvents have different densities: if the opposite-tube mass difference exceeds the centrifuge tolerance, run each solvent as its own run with dummy tubes, or trim fill volumes.

Loading and balancing

  • Use the rotor positions of the run sheet: adjacent masses face each other. Weigh every loaded tube; the sheet gives the expected mass.
  • Concentrated feeds are denser: the ΔP of each tube at the shared speed is listed — the spread is normally < 3 %.

Membrane, temperature, stabilisation

  • Six coupons from the same validated set (see the coupon-screening step of the Test Planner guide). Precondition in the process solvent as the module would be.
  • Bring feeds, cartridges and rotor to the batch temperature; viscosity and diffusivity change quickly with T, and at high concentration the feed viscosity matters.
  • Discard an initial permeate fraction (compaction, polarisation build-up), then collect the volume in the sheet. Keep the permeate small relative to the feed so that each tube stays at its batch state.

Reading the results

  • Weigh the permeate and record the time → flux. Measure cp of product (and impurity).
  • Enter them in "Enter measured results": the tool converts each tube to the module's flux and intrinsic rejection at that batch state and re-integrates the batch. Yield and time are then based on your membrane, not on the assumed rejection.
  • Compare the measured C·J curve with the predicted one: the concentration at its maximum is where to diafilter. If the maximum is at the highest concentration tested, the practical limit (solubility, viscosity, minimum tank volume) decides.
  • Watch the wall concentration column against solubility: precipitation on the membrane appears first at cm, not in the tank.

Replicates

  • Use "6 conditions" to see the shape of the response, "3 × 2" to confirm the critical points (start, knee, end) with replicates in opposite positions.

Procedure

  1. Precondition six coupons from the same validated set in the solvent; check cartridge compatibility.
  2. Prepare the six feeds by gravimetric dilution from a stock; measure and record each concentration.
  3. Set the centrifuge to — °C; bring feeds, cartridges and rotor to temperature.
  4. Fill each cartridge with V0, weigh it, load it in the position listed (opposite tubes balanced), spin at the speed above.
  5. Discard an initial permeate fraction, then collect the permeate volume listed; weigh it and record the time.
  6. Measure the permeate concentration of the product (and of the impurity).
  7. Enter volume, time and concentrations in the Batch Planner ("Enter measured results") to obtain the membrane's flux and rejection along the batch, the measured C·J optimum, and the re-integrated batch time and yield.
