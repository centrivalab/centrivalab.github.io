Model & validity

Element. 1-D plug flow along the feed channel, 60 cells per element. Mass transfer from Schock & Miquel (1987), Sh = 0.065 Re0.875 Sc0.25 with Re on the interstitial velocity and the spacer hydraulic diameter; channel friction λ = 6.23 Re−0.3. Film model cm = cp + (cb − cp) eJv/k with cp = (1 − Rint) cm. Osmotic pressure by van't Hoff (dilute solutions; for concentrated aqueous electrolytes use the Pitzer-based CP Calculator). Feed-channel cross-section Ach = A·h / 2L from the active area, element length and spacer thickness.

Cartridge. Concentration polarisation from the buoyancy-driven model of Geraldes et al. (2026), k = 0.28 U* 𝒢1/4 Sc−1/4, at the run-mean pressure and bulk concentration. The planner solves for the cartridge feed concentration whose run-mean wall concentration cm equals the area-mean cm of the element at the same ΔP. The same membrane at the same ΔP and cm gives the same flux, so the cartridge flux is taken equal to the element mean flux.

Properties. Solvent density and viscosity from handbook data (Andrade fit, 10–50 °C). Solute diffusivity by Stokes–Einstein with the molecular radius from molar mass and solute density; density contrast β = (1 − ρsolvent/ρsolute)/ρsolvent (volume-additive mixture). Both can be overridden by measured values.

Validity. The O-CMF correlation is dimensionless: it applies to any Newtonian solvent whenever the buoyancy parameter 𝒢, the Schmidt number and Jv/k lie in the ranges over which it was established (𝒢 7×10−9–5×10−6, Sc 700–2200, Jv/k ≤ 1.5). The planner checks each run and flags any group outside its range; Sc enters only as Sc−1/4, so a modest excursion in Sc is a short extrapolation. The property inputs — β (volume-additive mixture) and D (Stokes–Einstein) — are estimates: replace them by measured values when you have them. A quick in-system check: two runs at the same speed with feed concentrations differing by a factor of 3 should show the observed rejection rising with C0 as k ∝ C01/4 predicts. Solvents denser than the solute (DCM, chloroform, DMSO with most solutes) give β ≤ 0: the retained layer is stably stratified, the buoyancy model does not apply and the planner stops.

Rejection. A membrane's intrinsic rejection can depend on cm. That is why the planner reproduces cm instead of transferring a rejection measured elsewhere. When the element spans a wide range of cm (high recovery, several elements in series), the programme adds inlet, mid-length and outlet runs so the membrane is tested across that range.

Experimental guide

What the planner assumes about the run, and what to do on the bench so that the assumptions hold. Times and speeds for the preparation steps depend on your membrane and solvent — they are not calculated here.

Membrane preconditioning

  • Condition the membrane coupon in the test solvent the same way the element would be conditioned before service (solvent exchange, soaking, first-flush). The cartridge must hold the membrane in the same swollen/compacted state as the element.
  • For OSN membranes shipped in a preservative, exchange it completely before mounting.
  • Check the cartridge's solvent compatibility on the Support page.

Thermal equilibration

  • Set the centrifuge to the element temperature entered in the planner. Solvent viscosity and solute diffusivity change quickly with temperature, and the mass-transfer prediction uses the temperature you entered.
  • Bring feed, cartridge and rotor to that temperature before the run. A low-speed spin during equilibration is acceptable if it stays below the speed at which permeation starts — the Rotor Speed page gives the pressure at any speed.

Filtration stabilisation

  • The first permeate reflects compaction and the build-up of the polarised layer, not the steady state the planner describes. Discard an initial fraction, or collect in steps and use the later ones.
  • Keep the total permeate small relative to the feed (the planner limits it to 2 mL per run) so the feed concentration and the mean pressure stay close to the values the plan assumes. For more permeate, collect in steps and re-plan the speed for each.

Collection and timing

  • Stop at the permeate volume in the programme: it fixes both the mean pressure of the run and the recovery in the tube.
  • The time shown next to the volume is the estimated collection time from the predicted flux — it does not include preconditioning, equilibration or stabilisation. Weigh the permeate and record the actual time: they give you the measured flux.

Analysis and reading the result

  • Measure the permeate concentration cp and confirm the cartridge feed concentration.
  • Element rejection = 1 − cp / C0,element. Divide by the element feed concentration: the cartridge feed was chosen to give the same wall concentration, so the observed rejection in the cartridge is not the element's.
  • If the intrinsic rejection you measure (1 − cp/cm) differs from the value you entered, enter it and re-plan — cm moves a little with rejection.
  • Run two cartridges per condition.

Checking the model in your solvent

  • Run twice at the same speed with feed concentrations differing by a factor of three. The observed rejection should rise with concentration as the cartridge mass-transfer model predicts (k ∝ C01/4). If it does not, tell us. The correlation is dimensionless; the planner flags any run whose 𝒢, Sc or Jv/k falls outside the range over which it was established.
