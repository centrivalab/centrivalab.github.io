Spiral-Wound Test Planner

A membrane only responds to two things: the flux through it and the solute concentration at its surface. Describe the spiral-wound element you want to reproduce — size, feed flow, recovery, pressure, solvent and solute — and the planner returns the rotor speed and the feed concentration that expose the membrane in a CentrivaLab cartridge to the same conditions. The permeate you then measure is the permeate the element would give.

How to read the result.
  1. Prepare the feed at the concentration in the table (same solvent, same temperature as the element).
  2. Fill the cartridge with V0, spin at the speed in the table, stop at the permeate volume in the table.
  3. Measure the permeate concentration cp. The element rejection is 1 − cp / C0,element — divide by the element's feed concentration, not by the cartridge feed.
  4. If the measured rejection differs from the "expected rejection" you entered, enter the new value and re-plan: cm moves a little with rejection.

Model and validity

Element. 1-D plug flow along the feed channel, discretised in 60 cells per element. Mass transfer from Schock & Miquel (1987), Sh = 0.065 Re0.875 Sc0.25 with Re on the interstitial velocity and the spacer hydraulic diameter; channel friction λ = 6.23 Re−0.3. Film model cm = cp + (cb − cp) eJv/k with cp = (1 − Rint) cm. Osmotic pressure by van't Hoff (dilute solutions; for concentrated aqueous electrolytes use the Pitzer-based CP Calculator). The feed-channel cross-section is derived from the active area, the element length and the spacer thickness (Ach = A·h / 2L).

Cartridge. Concentration polarisation from the buoyancy-driven model of Geraldes et al. (2026), k = 0.28 U* 𝒢1/4 Sc−1/4, at the run-mean pressure and bulk concentration. The planner solves for the feed concentration whose run-mean cm equals the area-mean cm of the element at the same ΔP. Because the same membrane at the same ΔP and the same cm gives the same flux, the cartridge flux is taken equal to the element mean flux.

Properties. Solvent density and viscosity from handbook data (Andrade fit, 10–50 °C). Solute diffusivity by Stokes–Einstein with the molecular radius from molar mass and solute density; density contrast β = (1 − ρsolvent/ρsolute)/ρsolvent (volume-additive mixture). Both can be overridden or replaced by measured values.

Validity. The O-CMF correlation was validated with aqueous salts (Sc 700–2200, Jv/k ≤ 1.5). In organic solvents it is an extrapolation, flagged as such: confirm it in your system with two runs at the same speed and feed concentrations differing by a factor of 3 — the observed rejection should rise with C0 as k ∝ C01/4 predicts. Solvents denser than the solute (DCM, chloroform, DMSO with most solutes) give β ≤ 0: the retained layer is stably stratified, the buoyancy model does not apply and the planner stops.

Rejection. A membrane's intrinsic rejection can depend on cm. That is exactly why the planner reproduces cm instead of transferring a rejection measured elsewhere. When the element spans a wide range of cm (high recovery, several elements in series), the programme adds inlet, mid-length and outlet runs so the membrane is tested across that range.
