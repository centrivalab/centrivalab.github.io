Concentration Polarisation Calculator

Quick estimate of the polarisation factor Γ = cm/c0 − 1 at the membrane in a CentrivaLab cartridge. Choose the main solute (any cation + anion, or a neutral solute), its concentration, the initial feed and permeate volumes, the operating pressure and the permeate flux you expect — the tool returns the rotor speed, Γ, the wall concentration and the mass-transfer coefficient. Because the free surface recedes as permeate is collected, ΔP falls during a run at constant speed: the speed is set for the mean pressure between the start and the end of the collection. Add a dilute tracer (ion, salt or neutral) to get its polarisation too. No membrane parameters needed.

Model: Geraldes V., Fan H., Yang M., Rodrigues M., Elimelech M. — "Predicting concentration polarization in Ortho-Centrifugal Membrane Filters for NF/RO membrane screening" (2026): kc/U* = 0.28 𝒢1/4 Sc−1/4, U* = (amD)1/3, 𝒢A = RA βA cA0 Jv/UA* (Eq. 35–36); a dilute tracer B follows kB/UB* = 0.28 𝒢A1/4 ScB−1/4 (Eq. 47) — the convective field is set by A alone, B only contributes its own diffusivity. Electrolyte properties are estimated from ionic data: D from limiting ionic conductivities (Nernst–Hartley, with a Davies thermodynamic factor and a viscosity correction), viscosity from Jones–Dole ionic B-coefficients, β = (1/ρ) dρ/dc from conventional partial molar ionic volumes (Millero 1972); a trace ion in a supporting electrolyte uses its own ionic diffusivity. Neutral solutes: literature D° and partial molar volumes. All data at 25 °C (CRC Handbook; Jenkins & Marcus 1995), scaled to the set temperature. The framework was derived for neutral solutes; for electrolytes it is applied with the salt (Nernst–Hartley) diffusivity. For NaCl, MgSO₄ and lactose the device comparison uses measured property fits instead. Osmotic pressure π = φ ν m R T ρw with the osmotic coefficient φ from the Pitzer ion-interaction model (β⁽⁰⁾, β⁽¹⁾, β⁽²⁾, Cφ at 25 °C from Kim & Frederick, J. Chem. Eng. Data 33 (1988) 177, for ~140 salts; class-average parameters, flagged, otherwise) and φ = 1 + b·m for neutral solutes; it is used only to bound the input (πfeed ≤ 50 bar) and to check that the set flux is compatible with ΔP. Pressure: ΔP = ½ρω²(rm² − rs²) with the feed density ρ and the free-surface radius rs(V) from the cartridge calibration; since rs increases as the feed volume drops, the rotor speed is chosen for the arithmetic mean of ΔP at the initial volume V0 and at the final volume V0 − Vp (within ~0.3 bar of the time-averaged pressure for Vp up to ~half of V0).
