Concentration Polarisation Predictor

Quick estimate of the polarisation factor Γ = cm/c0 − 1 at the membrane in a CentrivaLab cartridge. Choose the main solute (any cation + anion, or a neutral solute), its concentration, the operating pressure and the permeate flux you expect — the tool returns Γ, the wall concentration and the mass-transfer coefficient. Add a dilute tracer (ion, salt or neutral) to get its polarisation too. No membrane parameters needed.

Model: Geraldes V., Fan H., Yang M., Rodrigues M., Elimelech M. — "Predicting concentration polarization in Ortho-Centrifugal Membrane Filters for NF/RO membrane screening" (2026): kc/U* = 0.28 𝒢1/4 Sc−1/4, U* = (amD)1/3, 𝒢A = RA βA cA0 Jv/UA* (Eq. 35–36); a dilute tracer B follows kB/UB* = 0.28 𝒢A1/4 ScB−1/4 (Eq. 47) — the convective field is set by A alone, B only contributes its own diffusivity. Electrolyte properties are estimated from ionic data: D from limiting ionic conductivities (Nernst–Hartley, with a Davies thermodynamic factor and a viscosity correction), viscosity from Jones–Dole ionic B-coefficients, β = (1/ρ) dρ/dc from conventional partial molar ionic volumes (Millero 1972); a trace ion in a supporting electrolyte uses its own ionic diffusivity. Neutral solutes: literature D° and partial molar volumes. All data at 25 °C (CRC Handbook; Jenkins & Marcus 1995), scaled to the set temperature. The framework was derived for neutral solutes; for electrolytes it is applied with the salt (Nernst–Hartley) diffusivity. For NaCl, MgSO₄ and lactose the CP simulator uses measured property fits instead.
